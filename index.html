The Week in Context

What the stories are telling us together

Commercial awareness is more than remembering headlines. The useful skill is seeing the connections between markets, regulation, capital and the decisions businesses are making.

Interview lens

When asked what you have been following, start with a theme rather than a headline. Then use two or three stories as evidence for your argument.

Example: “One theme I've noticed is that infrastructure and regulation are increasingly shaping the economics of investment in Ireland. You can see that in data centres, housing and energy assets, but also in banking and technology transactions where regulatory approval directly affects deal certainty.”

How to use this in an interview

A strong commercial-awareness answer usually has five steps: headline → mechanism → affected parties → client impact → legal implications.

Do not list six unrelated headlines. Choose one theme and use two or three developments as evidence that you understand the wider picture.
